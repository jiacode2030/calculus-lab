Infinite limits & end behavior

What happens when the function blows up near a point, or when x gets enormous? Both questions give asymptotes.

The idea

A vertical asymptote at x = a means the function grows without bound as x → a: lim f(x) = ±∞ from at least one side. In a fraction it happens where the bottom is 0 but the top is not. The limit "= ∞" is shorthand: it technically does not exist, but we say how it fails.

A horizontal asymptote comes from limits at infinity: limx→∞ f(x) = L. For a ratio of polynomials compare the leading terms: a lower-degree top gives 0, equal degrees give the ratio of leading coefficients, a higher-degree top gives ±∞ (no horizontal asymptote).

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose equal degrees and zoom out. What height does the graph flatten to?

    What you should see

    Toward 2, the ratio of leading coefficients (2x² over x²). The two vertical asymptotes at x = ±1 stay put while the ends level off.

  2. Choose top higher degree and zoom out. Does it level off?

    What you should see

    No. It keeps growing, like the line y = x. A higher-degree top means no horizontal asymptote: the limit at infinity is ±∞.

  3. Choose sin x / x and zoom out. The ends go to 0. Does the graph ever touch y = 0?

    What you should see

    Yes, again and again: sin x / x = 0 at x = π, 2π, 3π, … while the waves shrink toward 0. A graph may cross its horizontal asymptote; the asymptote only describes where the far ends go.

  4. Choose top lower degree and zoom out. Which height do the ends approach?

    What you should see

    0: (3x + 1)/(x² − 4) has the higher degree on the bottom, so the bottom wins. The vertical asymptotes x = ±2 stay put.

Worked example

Find the asymptotes of f(x) = (3x² − 3)/(x² − 4).

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Vertical. The bottom is 0 at x = ±2. The top at x = ±2 is 9, not 0, so both are vertical asymptotes.
  2. Horizontal. The degrees match (2 and 2): divide the leading coefficients, 3/1 = 3.
  3. Check the limit. limx→∞ (3 − 3/x²)/(1 − 4/x²) = 3/1 = 3. The same value at −∞.
  4. Answer. Vertical: x = 2 and x = −2. Horizontal: y = 3.

Common mistakes

  • Calling every zero of the bottom a vertical asymptote. If the factor also cancels with the top, it is a hole.
  • Writing lim = ∞ as if ∞ were a number you can substitute.
  • Using only the leading coefficient of the top for the horizontal asymptote.
  • Forgetting the limit at −∞ can differ from +∞ (for example, ex or an odd-degree ratio).
  • Thinking a graph cannot cross its horizontal asymptote. It can, near the origin; the asymptote describes the far ends.

Reading the sign

To get the sign of an infinite limit, check which side you approach from. Near x = 2, 1/(x − 2) is large positive from the right (positive denominator) and large negative from the left, so limx→2⁺ = ∞ and limx→2⁻ = −∞, and the two-sided limit does not exist.
