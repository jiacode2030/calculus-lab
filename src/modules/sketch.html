Connecting f, f′ and f″

The three graphs tell one story. Draw the slope and you can rebuild the shape of the function.

The idea

Think of f′ as the slope-meter for f, and f″ as the slope-meter for f′. Where f′ is above the axis, f rises; where f′ crosses from above to below, f has a peak; where f′ has a peak or valley, f has an inflection point; where f′ is rising, f is concave up.

On the exam you read this information from a given graph (of f, f′ or f″) and justify a conclusion. In the lab you control f′ with draggable points, and f is rebuilt from it. Drag the nodes and watch the extrema and inflections move.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Drag the middle point of f′ well above the axis. What happens to f?

    What you should see

    f rises steeply where f′ is large and positive, and the bends of f follow the ups and downs of f′.

  2. Make f′ cross the axis downward. What does f do there?

    What you should see

    f has a local maximum where f′ goes from positive to negative. Drag until you see the peak.

Worked example

The graph of f′ is positive on (−3, 1), negative on (1, 4) and has a minimum at x = 2.5. Describe f.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Increasing/decreasing. f′ > 0 on (−3, 1): f increasing there; f′ < 0 on (1, 4): f decreasing.
  2. Extremum. f′ changes + → − at x = 1, so f has a local maximum at x = 1.
  3. Concavity. f′ is decreasing before its minimum at 2.5 and increasing after, so f″ changes sign at 2.5.
  4. Answer. Local maximum at x = 1, and an inflection point at x = 2.5 (concave down before, concave up after).

Common mistakes

  • Reading f′ as if it were f. A positive f′ means rising, not "positive height".
  • Looking at where f′ is zero for inflection. Inflection is where f′ has an extremum (f″ changes sign).
  • Forgetting f is only determined up to a vertical shift.
  • Confusing which graph is given: always read the label.
  • Not justifying: say "f′ changes sign from + to − at x = 1".

Justifications that earn the point

"f has a relative maximum at x = c because f′ changes from positive to negative at x = c." "f has a point of inflection at x = c because f″ changes sign at x = c." Name the derivative and the sign change.
