Parametric equations

Instead of y as a function of x, let both depend on time t. The curve is the path of a moving point, and slopes come from the chain rule.

The idea

A parametric curve is a pair x = x(t), y = y(t). As t increases the point (x, y) traces a path, which can loop, double back and cross itself, none of which a function y = f(x) can do. Eliminating t sometimes gives a familiar curve (a circle, an ellipse), but the parametrization also records the direction and speed of travel.

The slope comes from the chain rule: dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt), valid where dx/dt ≠ 0. A horizontal tangent happens where dy/dt = 0 (and dx/dt ≠ 0), a vertical tangent where dx/dt = 0 (and dy/dt ≠ 0). The second derivative is d²y/dx² = (d/dt(dy/dx))/(dx/dt). The arc length is ∫ √((dx/dt)² + (dy/dt)²) dt.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. On the ellipse, find t where the tangent is horizontal.

    What you should see

    At t = π/2, dy/dt = 2cos t = 0 while dx/dt = −3 sin t ≠ 0: the top of the ellipse, a horizontal tangent.

  2. On the cycloid, put t at 2π. What is happening to the slope?

    What you should see

    Both dx/dt = 1 − cos t and dy/dt = sin t are 0: a cusp, where the wheel point touches down. The slope is undefined there.

Worked example

For x = t² and y = t³ − 3t, find dy/dx and d²y/dx² at t = 2, and the points of horizontal tangent.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Rates. dx/dt = 2t, dy/dt = 3t² − 3.
  2. Slope. dy/dx = (3t² − 3)/(2t). At t = 2: 9/4.
  3. Second derivative. d/dt(dy/dx) = d/dt[(3/2)t − (3/2)/t] = 3/2 + (3/2)/t². Divide by dx/dt = 2t: at t = 2 that is (3/2 + 3/8)/4 = 15/32.
  4. Horizontal tangents. dy/dt = 0 at t = ±1 (dx/dt = ±2 ≠ 0): the points (1, −2) and (1, 2).
  5. Answer. dy/dx = 9/4, d²y/dx² = 15/32; horizontal tangents at (1, −2) and (1, 2).

Common mistakes

  • Differentiating dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt) with respect to t and forgetting to divide by dx/dt again for the second derivative.
  • Writing dy/dx = dx/dt ÷ dy/dt (upside down).
  • Calling a point with dx/dt = dy/dt = 0 a tangent: check, it may be a cusp.
  • Forgetting that t is time, not the x-coordinate.
  • Using speed formula without the square root.

Speed and direction

The velocity vector is ⟨dx/dt, dy/dt⟩ and always points along the tangent in the direction of travel. The speed is its magnitude √((dx/dt)² + (dy/dt)²). The distance traveled is the integral of speed, which is the arc length formula.
