Average value & motion with integrals

The average of a function over an interval is the height of a rectangle with the same area. And for a moving particle, integrals tell you how far it went and how far it ended up.

The idea

The average value of f on [a, b] is favg = (1/(b − a)) ∫ab f(x) dx: total accumulation divided by the length of the interval. A continuous function actually equals its average somewhere (the Mean Value Theorem for integrals).

For a particle with velocity v(t): displacement (net change in position) is ∫ v dt, while total distance traveled is ∫ |v| dt. If v changes sign the two differ. And position is s(T) = s(0) + ∫0T v dt.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. On x² over [0, 3], find the average value and where f equals it.

    What you should see

    ∫₀³ x² dx = 9, so the average is 3. x² = 3 at x = √3 ≈ 1.73: the green dot where the curve crosses the average line.

  2. Choose 4x − x² on [0, 4]. Predict the average before you look. How many points equal it?

    What you should see

    ∫₀⁴ (4x − x²) dx = 32 − 64/3 = 32/3, so the average is 8/3 ≈ 2.667, two thirds of the peak height 4. The curve crosses the average twice, at x = 2 ± 2/√3 ≈ 0.845 and 3.155. The theorem promises at least one such point, not exactly one.

  3. Set T = 3 on the motion graph. Compare displacement and distance.

    What you should see

    Displacement is 0 (the signed areas cancel): the particle ends where it started, s(3) = 2 m. But the distance is 4/3 + 4/3 = 8/3 m: it went forward, then back. Distance counts both trips.

  4. Now slide T to 5. Find displacement, distance and position.

    What you should see

    Displacement 20/3 ≈ 6.667 m and distance 28/3 ≈ 9.333 m: the extra 8/3 is the out-and-back trip from t = 0 to 3. Position s(5) = 2 + 20/3 = 26/3 ≈ 8.667 m. After t = 3 the particle only moves forward, so the two grow at the same rate.

Worked example

Find the average value of f(x) = x² on [0, 3], and the displacement and distance for v(t) = t² − 4t + 3 on [0, 3].

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Average value. (1/3) ∫03 x² dx = (1/3)(9) = 3.
  2. Displacement. ∫03 (t² − 4t + 3) dt = [t³/3 − 2t² + 3t]03 = 9 − 18 + 9 = 0.
  3. Sign of v. v = (t − 1)(t − 3): positive on [0, 1), negative on (1, 3).
  4. Distance. ∫01 v dt = 4/3, and ∫13 v dt = −4/3. Distance = 4/3 + 4/3 = 8/3.
  5. Answer. Average value 3; displacement 0; total distance 8/3 m.

Worked example 2: a day's temperature (AP-style)

The temperature in a town, in degrees Fahrenheit, is modeled by T(t) = 60 − 10 cos(πt/12), where t is hours after midnight. (a) Find the average temperature from 6 am (t = 6) to 6 pm (t = 18). (b) At what times between 6 am and 6 pm does the temperature equal that average? (c) Find the average rate of change of T from t = 6 to t = 12, with units. How is it different from (a)?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. (a) Set up. Average = (1/(18 − 6)) ∫618 T(t) dt.
  2. Integrate. An antiderivative is 60t − (120/π) sin(πt/12). So ∫618 T dt = 60(12) − (120/π)(sin(3π/2) − sin(π/2)) = 720 + 240/π.
  3. Divide by 12. Average = 60 + 20/π ≈ 66.366 °F.
  4. (b) Solve 60 − 10 cos(πt/12) = 60 + 20/π: cos(πt/12) = −2/π, so t ≈ 8.636 and 15.364 (about 8:38 am and 3:22 pm). On a calculator-active question, solving T(t) = 66.366 numerically is enough.
  5. (c) (T(12) − T(6))/(12 − 6) = (70 − 60)/6 ≈ 1.667 °F per hour. This is a rate (degrees per hour, the slope of a secant), while (a) is a temperature (degrees, the height of the equal-area rectangle). Do not mix them up.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by the wrong thing: divide by b − a, not by b or by 2.
  • Using ∫ v instead of ∫ |v| for distance.
  • Forgetting the starting position when finding position.
  • Averaging endpoint values (f(a) + f(b))/2 instead of integrating.
  • Not splitting the integral where v changes sign.
  • Confusing the average value of f, (1/(b − a))∫ f, with the average rate of change of f, (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a).

Units

Average value has the units of f. Displacement and distance have the units of v × t. If f is a rate, ∫ f is a total amount; dividing by the interval length gives the average rate.
