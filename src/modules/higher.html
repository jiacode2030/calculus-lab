Higher-order derivatives & choosing a rule

Differentiate again and you get the rate at which the slope changes. Then practise the skill that matters most on the exam: picking the right rule.

The idea

The second derivative f″ is the derivative of f′. If f is position, f′ is velocity and f″ is acceleration. If f′ is slope, f″ tells you how the slope is changing: positive means the slope is increasing (the curve bends up), negative means it bends down. You will use this in Unit 5 to find concavity.

With more rules, the real skill is choosing. Look at the outermost operation first: is the whole expression a sum, a product, a quotient, or a function of something? That operation is the rule you apply first.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose x³ − 3x and slide x to 0. What is f″ there, and what does the curve do?

    What you should see

    f″(0) = 0: the bend switches there. The curve changes from bending down (f″ < 0 for x < 0) to bending up (f″ > 0).

  2. Choose sin x. Where is f″ largest in size?

    What you should see

    At the peak (x = π/2): f″ = −sin(π/2) = −1. The curve bends sharply there, and f′ passes through zero.

Worked example

Find f″(x) for f(x) = x e2x.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Choose a rule. It is a product of x and e2x.
  2. First derivative. f′ = 1·e2x + x·(2e2x) = e2x(1 + 2x).
  3. Differentiate again (product). f″ = 2e2x(1 + 2x) + e2x·2.
  4. Simplify. f″ = e2x(2 + 4x + 2) = e2x(4x + 4).

Common mistakes

  • Differentiating the original instead of f′ for the second derivative.
  • Not simplifying f′ before differentiating again.
  • Choosing the wrong rule: look at the outermost operation, not the innermost.
  • Confusing f² with f″.
  • Sign errors when differentiating trig functions twice: sin → cos → −sin.

Which rule first?

Sum or difference: split and differentiate each term. Product of two functions of x: product rule. Quotient: quotient rule (or rewrite as a product with a negative power). A function of a function, like sin(3x) or √(x² + 1): chain rule. Constant times something: pull out the constant. Many problems need two or three of these in order.
