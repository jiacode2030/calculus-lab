Optimization

Biggest area, least material, fastest time: set up a formula for the quantity, find where its derivative is zero, and check it is the best.

The idea

An optimization problem has four steps. Draw and name the variables. Write the objective (the thing to maximize or minimize) and a constraint (what is fixed). Use the constraint to write the objective in one variable. Then differentiate, set to zero, solve, and justify that you have a max or min (first or second derivative test, or the candidates test on a closed interval).

Do not forget the domain: dimensions are positive, and endpoints can be the answer. In the lab you will see the objective graph; the best value sits at a peak or valley of the graph, where its derivative (green) crosses zero.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose the fence problem. Slide x to find the largest area.

    What you should see

    The maximum area is 1250 ft² at x = 25 (so the other side is 50). The derivative A′ = 100 − 4x is zero there and changes from + to −.

  2. Choose the open box. What size square cut gives the largest volume?

    What you should see

    x = 2 gives V = 2(12 − 4)² = 128 cm³, the maximum for a 12 cm sheet. Any larger cut leaves too little base.

Worked example

100 ft of fence encloses a rectangular pen against a straight river (no fence on the river side). Find the dimensions of the largest area.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Variables. Let x = the two sides perpendicular to the river, y = the side parallel. Constraint: 2x + y = 100, so y = 100 − 2x.
  2. Objective. A = x·y = x(100 − 2x) = 100x − 2x², with 0 < x < 50.
  3. Differentiate. A′ = 100 − 4x = 0, so x = 25.
  4. Justify. A″ = −4 < 0, so x = 25 is a maximum.
  5. Answer. x = 25 ft and y = 50 ft, giving the maximum area 1250 ft².

Common mistakes

  • Not using the constraint to reduce to one variable.
  • Forgetting to check endpoints or the domain.
  • Giving the optimal x when asked for the maximum value (or the reverse).
  • Not justifying that it is a max or min.
  • Mixing units, for example using a formula with radius when the problem gives diameter.

Common setups

Perimeter fixed, maximize area: the square wins. Area fixed, minimize perimeter: the square. Volume fixed, minimize surface area of a can: height = diameter. Distance to a curve: minimize the square of the distance (the same minimum, no square root).
