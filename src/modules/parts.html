Integration by parts

The product rule, run backwards. It trades a hard integral of a product for a (hopefully easier) one.

The idea

The product rule says (uv)′ = u′v + uv′. Integrate both sides and rearrange: ∫ u dv = uv − ∫ v du. You split the integrand into a part u to differentiate and a part dv to integrate. It pays off when differentiating u makes it simpler (x becomes 1, ln x becomes 1/x) and integrating dv is easy (eˣ, sin x, cos x).

A good rule of thumb for choosing u is LIATE: Logarithmic, Inverse trig, Algebraic (powers of x), Trig, Exponential: pick u as the one that comes first. Some integrals need parts twice (x²eˣ), and a few circle back to themselves (eˣ sin x), which you solve algebraically.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose ∫ x eˣ dx. Which choice of u makes the new integral easier: u = x or u = eˣ? Think, then read the formula line.

    What you should see

    u = x: then du = dx and v = eˣ, so ∫ v du = ∫ eˣ dx, trivial. With u = eˣ you would get ∫ (x²/2) eˣ dx, which is harder than the original.

  2. Choose ∫ ln x dx. There is only one function; where is the product?

    What you should see

    Write ln x = ln x · 1: u = ln x, dv = dx. Then du = dx/x, v = x, and ∫ ln x dx = x ln x − ∫ 1 dx = x ln x − x.

Worked example

Evaluate ∫ x ex dx and ∫01 x ex dx.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Choose u and dv (LIATE). u = x (algebraic), dv = eˣ dx.
  2. Differentiate and integrate. du = dx, v = eˣ.
  3. Apply the formula. ∫ x eˣ dx = x eˣ − ∫ eˣ dx = x eˣ − eˣ + C.
  4. Definite integral. [x eˣ − eˣ]01 = (e − e) − (0 − 1) = 1.
  5. Answer. (x − 1)eˣ + C, and the definite integral is 1.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing u badly (making the new integral harder). Try the other choice.
  • Forgetting to subtract ∫ v du, or its sign.
  • Dropping the product uv in a definite integral: evaluate [uv] at the limits too.
  • Not recognizing ln x needs u = ln x, dv = dx.
  • Forgetting + C in an indefinite integral.

Twice, or circular

For ∫ x² eˣ dx, apply parts twice: the power of x drops from 2 to 1 to 0. For ∫ eˣ sin x dx, applying parts twice returns the original integral: I = (…) − I, so I = (…)/2. The tabular method (a column of derivatives of u and a column of integrals of dv) organizes repeated parts.
