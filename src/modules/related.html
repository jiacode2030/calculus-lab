Related rates

When two quantities are tied together by an equation and both change in time, their rates are tied together too.

The idea

A ladder slides down a wall. The foot moves out at a steady 2 ft/s, but the top does not move down at a steady rate: it starts slowly and speeds up. The lengths x and y are linked by x² + y² = L², so their rates are linked by the derivative of that equation with respect to time.

The method: draw and label; write an equation that holds at every moment; differentiate with respect to t (chain rule on every variable); only then substitute the numbers of the moment you care about. Substituting before differentiating freezes the variables and gives 0.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. On the ladder (10 ft, foot moving out at 2 ft/s) set x = 6. How fast is the top sliding down?

    What you should see

    y = 8, and dy/dt = −(x/y)(2) = −(6/8)(2) = −1.5 ft/s: the top drops 1.5 ft/s.

  2. Now slide x up to 9. What happens to the speed of the top?

    What you should see

    y ≈ 4.36 and dy/dt ≈ −4.13 ft/s: it speeds up a lot as the ladder nears the floor, even though the foot moves at a steady 2 ft/s.

Worked example

A 10 ft ladder leans on a wall. Its foot slides away at 2 ft/s. How fast is the top sliding down when the foot is 6 ft from the wall?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Draw and equation. x = distance of foot from wall, y = height of top: x² + y² = 100.
  2. Differentiate with respect to t. 2x·dx/dt + 2y·dy/dt = 0, so dy/dt = −(x/y)·dx/dt.
  3. Substitute the moment. x = 6 gives y = √(100 − 36) = 8, and dx/dt = 2.
  4. Compute. dy/dt = −(6/8)(2) = −1.5.
  5. Answer. The top is sliding down at 1.5 ft/s (dy/dt = −1.5 ft/s).

Common mistakes

  • Substituting numbers before differentiating. Constants have derivative 0, so you lose the rates.
  • Forgetting dx/dt: 2x alone is not d/dt of x².
  • Treating a changing quantity as constant (for example the ladder's length is constant, but x and y are not).
  • Ignoring signs: a decreasing quantity has a negative rate. Report it with the right direction.
  • Dropping units. Rates have units of quantity per time.
