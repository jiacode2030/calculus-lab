u-substitution

The chain rule run backwards: when an integral contains a function and its derivative, rename the inside as u and the integral becomes simple.

The idea

The chain rule gave d/dx f(g(x)) = f′(g(x))·g′(x). Reverse it: ∫ f′(g(x))·g′(x) dx = f(g(x)) + C. In practice, look for an inside function whose derivative also appears: in ∫ 2x cos(x²) dx the inside is x² and 2x is its derivative. Let u = x², so du = 2x dx, and the integral becomes ∫ cos u du = sin u + C.

For a definite integral either change back to x at the end, or (better) change the limits: when x goes from a to b, u goes from g(a) to g(b). The areas are equal, which the lab shows.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose 2x cos(x²). What are the new limits when b = 1?

    What you should see

    u = x², so u runs from 0² = 0 to 1² = 1. The right graph is cos u on [0, 1]; both areas equal sin 1 ≈ 0.841.

  2. Choose x/(x²+1). Why u = x² + 1?

    What you should see

    Its derivative 2x is almost the numerator x (off by a factor of 2), so x dx = du/2 and the integral is (1/2)∫ du/u = (1/2) ln u.

  3. Choose x·eˣ² with b = 1. What is the area, and what are the new limits?

    What you should see

    u = x², so u goes from 0 to 1 and x dx = du/2: the area is (1/2)∫01 eᵘ du = (e − 1)/2 ≈ 0.859.

  4. Choose 2x cos(x²) and slide b to 1.6. Why is the area smaller than at b = 1?

    What you should see

    The area is sin(b²) = sin 2.56 ≈ 0.549, less than sin 1 ≈ 0.841. Once x² passes π/2 ≈ 1.57 (x ≈ 1.25) the integrand cos(x²) is negative, so the area starts to cancel. The picture in u shows cos u on [0, 2.56].

Worked example

Evaluate ∫02 x(x² + 1)³ dx.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Choose u. The inside is x² + 1, whose derivative 2x nearly matches the extra factor x. Let u = x² + 1.
  2. Find du. du = 2x dx, so x dx = du/2.
  3. Change the limits. x = 0 → u = 1; x = 2 → u = 5.
  4. Rewrite and integrate. ∫15 u³ (du/2) = (1/2)(u⁴/4) |15 = (1/8)(625 − 1).
  5. Answer. 624/8 = 78.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to change the limits (or to change back to x at the end).
  • Leaving some x in the integral after substituting. Everything must be in u.
  • Not adjusting for a constant: if du = 2x dx you must replace x dx by du/2.
  • Choosing u that does not make the rest cancel: u should be the inside whose derivative appears.
  • Dropping + C in an indefinite integral.

Choosing u

Good candidates: the inside of a power (3x + 1)⁵, the exponent of e, the argument of sin or cos, the denominator of a fraction when its derivative is in the numerator, or the inside of a square root. If a constant factor is missing, it is fine: just divide by it.
