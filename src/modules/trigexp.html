Trig, exponentials & logs

Sine, cosine, ex and ln x: four functions you will differentiate and integrate constantly. Here is what each one looks like and why.

The idea

On the unit circle, the point at angle θ (measured in radians from the positive x-axis) is (cos θ, sin θ). That one picture gives you the graphs of sine and cosine, their values at the special angles, and the identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. Calculus uses radians because then the arc length equals the angle.

An exponential bx multiplies by b for every step in x. The special base e ≈ 2.718 is the one whose slope equals its height at every point. Its inverse is the natural log ln x: ln(ex) = x and eln x = x.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Set θ = π/4 (about 0.785). What are sine and cosine?

    What you should see

    Both are √2/2 ≈ 0.707: at 45° the point sits on the diagonal, so its x and y coordinates are equal.

  2. Slide the base b to e (2.718). What is the slope of bx at x = 0?

    What you should see

    1. For other bases the slope at 0 is ln b: below 1 for b < e, above 1 for b > e. Only e makes the slope exactly equal to the height (1) at x = 0.

  3. Set θ = 3π/2 (about 4.71). What are sin θ and cos θ, and where is the point?

    What you should see

    sin θ ≈ −1 and cos θ ≈ 0: the point is at the bottom of the circle, (0, −1). The angle is measured counter-clockwise from the positive x-axis, and tan θ blows up here because cos θ = 0.

  4. Set the base b = 2, then b = 5. What is the slope of bˣ at x = 0 each time?

    What you should see

    For b = 2 the slope at 0 is ln 2 ≈ 0.693, and for b = 5 it is ln 5 ≈ 1.609. The slope of bˣ at x = 0 is always ln b, so it equals 1 only for b = e.

Worked example

Solve e2x = 7 for x.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Undo e with ln. Take the natural log of both sides: ln(e2x) = ln 7.
  2. Simplify. ln(e2x) = 2x, so 2x = ln 7.
  3. Solve. x = (ln 7)/2.
  4. Answer. x ≈ 0.973. Check: e1.946 ≈ 7.

Common mistakes

  • Using degrees in calculus formulas. Set your calculator to radians.
  • Writing ln(a + b) = ln a + ln b. The rule is for products: ln(ab) = ln a + ln b.
  • Forgetting ln x is only defined for x > 0.
  • Thinking ex can be zero or negative: it is always positive.
  • Mixing up sin²θ with sin(θ²).

The special angles

Memorize the first-quadrant values: sin 0 = 0, sin π/6 = 1/2, sin π/4 = √2/2, sin π/3 = √3/2, sin π/2 = 1, and cosine runs the other way (1, √3/2, √2/2, 1/2, 0). The signs in the other quadrants follow from which coordinate is negative.
