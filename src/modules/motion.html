Derivatives in context & motion

A derivative is a rate, and every rate has units and a story. The classic story: a particle moving along a line.

The idea

If s(t) is position, then v(t) = s′(t) is velocity and a(t) = v′(t) = s″(t) is acceleration. Velocity can be negative (moving left or down). Speed is the size of velocity, |v|. The units of each derivative are the units of the quantity divided by the units of time: meters, meters per second, meters per second squared.

The particle is at rest when v = 0, and it can turn around only there. It is speeding up when velocity and acceleration have the same sign, and slowing down when the signs are opposite. This is the sign test the exam loves.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose the first motion and set t = 2.5. Is the particle speeding up or slowing down?

    What you should see

    v(2.5) = 3(1.5)(−0.5) = −2.25 and a(2.5) = 6(2.5) − 12 = 3. Opposite signs, so it is slowing down: moving left, but being pushed right.

  2. Choose the ball (s = 20t − 5t²) and set t = 2. What does v = 0 mean here?

    What you should see

    At t = 2 the ball is at its highest point (s = 20): it has stopped for an instant before falling. Velocity changes sign there, and acceleration is −10 the whole time.

Worked example

A particle has position s(t) = t³ − 6t² + 9t for t ≥ 0. When is it at rest, and is it speeding up or slowing down at t = 2.5?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Velocity. v(t) = s′(t) = 3t² − 12t + 9 = 3(t − 1)(t − 3).
  2. At rest. v = 0 at t = 1 and t = 3.
  3. Acceleration. a(t) = v′(t) = 6t − 12.
  4. Signs at t = 2.5. v(2.5) = 3(1.5)(−0.5) = −2.25 and a(2.5) = 3.
  5. Answer. At rest at t = 1 and t = 3. At t = 2.5 velocity is negative and acceleration positive (opposite signs), so the particle is slowing down.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing velocity with speed: velocity has a sign, speed does not.
  • Saying the particle is speeding up whenever acceleration is positive. You must compare it with the sign of velocity.
  • Forgetting units. Always write them: m/s, m/s².
  • Treating v = 0 as a stop forever. It may only be a turning point.
  • Using the position formula to find velocity at a time without differentiating first.

Reading the story

Positive velocity means moving in the positive direction; negative means the opposite. Where velocity changes sign the particle turns around. Where acceleration is zero velocity is momentarily constant (a maximum or minimum of v). The same logic reads any rate: if V(t) is volume, V′(t) is a flow rate, and V′ > 0 means filling.
