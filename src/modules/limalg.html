Algebraic limits

When plugging in gives 0/0, the function is hiding something. Algebra rewrites it into a form that does not hide it.

The idea

Direct substitution works whenever the function is continuous at a: just evaluate. The trouble is the form 0/0, which is not a number but a clue: the top and bottom share a factor that is going to zero. Remove the shared factor and what is left can be evaluated.

Three moves cover almost everything. Factor and cancel (polynomials). Multiply by the conjugate (square roots). Combine fractions (a fraction inside a fraction). When a function is trapped between two others that share a limit, the squeeze theorem gives the answer.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose Factor. The orange dashed line is the simplified formula. Where do the two graphs differ?

    What you should see

    Only at x = 3, where the original has a hole. Everywhere else they are the same graph, so they approach the same height: the simplified formula gives the limit.

  2. Choose Squeeze. Why can't you just plug in x = 0?

    What you should see

    sin(1/0) is meaningless. But sin is always between −1 and 1, so x²·sin(1/x) is trapped between −x² and x². Both bounds go to 0, so the function is squeezed to 0.

  3. Choose Conjugate. What happens if you plug in x = 0, and what does the dashed formula give?

    What you should see

    Plugging in gives 0/0. Multiplying top and bottom by √(x + 4) + 2 leaves 1/(√(x + 4) + 2), which is 1/4 at x = 0. Check: at x = 0.1 the original is (√4.1 − 2)/0.1 ≈ 0.2485, already close to 0.25.

  4. Choose Fractions. Why is the limit negative?

    What you should see

    Combining 1/x − 1/3 = (3 − x)/(3x) gives (3 − x)/(3x(x − 3)) = −1/(3x) once the factor is cancelled. At x = 3 that is −1/9 ≈ −0.111: the numerator 3 − x is the opposite sign of the denominator x − 3.

Worked example

Find limx→2 (x² − x − 2)/(x − 2).

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Plug in. 4 − 2 − 2 = 0 on top and 2 − 2 = 0 on the bottom: the form 0/0, so keep working.
  2. Factor the top. x² − x − 2 = (x − 2)(x + 1).
  3. Cancel the common factor. For x ≠ 2, (x − 2)(x + 1)/(x − 2) = x + 1. The limit only looks at x ≠ 2, so this is allowed.
  4. Substitute. limx→2 (x + 1) = 3.
  5. Answer. The limit is 3. The original has a hole at (2, 3).

Common mistakes

  • Cancelling a factor that does not go to 0: only cancel the factor that is the cause of 0/0.
  • Stopping at 0/0 and writing "the limit does not exist". 0/0 means "do more work".
  • Multiplying only the top by the conjugate. Multiply top and bottom by the same thing.
  • Using the squeeze theorem with bounds that do not have the same limit.
  • Writing "= 3" after the first step. Keep "lim" until you substitute.

Why algebra is allowed

Two functions that agree everywhere except at a have the same limit at a, because a limit never looks at x = a itself. That is why cancelling (x − 2)/(x − 2) is legal in a limit, even though 0/0 at x = 2 is not defined.
