The definite integral & the Fundamental Theorem

Differentiating and integrating are opposites. This theorem is why antiderivatives can find areas.

The idea

Define the accumulation function A(x) = ∫ax f(t) dt: the signed area from a up to x. Slide x to the right and the area grows at a rate equal to the height of f at the edge. That is FTC Part 1: A′(x) = f(x). Integration undoes differentiation.

FTC Part 2 turns that into a method: if F is any antiderivative of f, then ∫ab f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a). No more adding rectangles. A related fact: the net change of a quantity equals the integral of its rate, ∫ab f′(x) dx = f(b) − f(a).

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose t² − 2t and slide x to 2. What is happening to A?

    What you should see

    A is at its lowest point (A(2) = −4/3): f is negative until x = 2 (area below the axis counts negative), so A decreases, and f(2) = 0 where A turns around.

  2. Compare f(x) with the slope of A at several x. What do you see?

    What you should see

    They are equal everywhere: the slope of the accumulation graph is the height of f. That is FTC Part 1.

Worked example

Evaluate ∫13 (2x + 1) dx, and find d/dx ∫0x² sin t dt.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Antiderivative. F(x) = x² + x.
  2. FTC Part 2. F(3) − F(1) = (9 + 3) − (1 + 1) = 10. The integral is 10.
  3. Second problem: chain rule. The upper limit is g(x) = x², so d/dx ∫0x² sin t dt = sin(x²)·(x²)′.
  4. Answer. 2x sin(x²).

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to subtract: F(b) − F(a), not F(b) + F(a).
  • Adding + C in a definite integral. It cancels, so leave it out.
  • Forgetting the chain rule factor g′(x) when the upper limit is not just x.
  • Treating area below the axis as positive. The integral is net (signed) area.
  • Swapping limits without changing the sign: ∫ba = −∫ab.

Properties worth knowing

∫aa f = 0; ∫ab f = −∫ba f; ∫ac f = ∫ab f + ∫bc f; constants pull out and sums split. If f is the rate of change of a quantity, ∫ab f is the total change in that quantity from a to b.
