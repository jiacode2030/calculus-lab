Logistic models

Real populations do not grow forever. A logistic model grows fast when small, slows as it approaches a carrying capacity, and levels off.

The idea

dP/dt = kP(1 − P/L). When P is small the factor (1 − P/L) is near 1, so growth is almost exponential. As P approaches the carrying capacity L, the factor shrinks to 0 and growth stops. P = 0 and P = L are equilibria; L is stable. The solution is an S-shaped curve with limit L.

The growth rate dP/dt is a parabola in P: zero at P = 0 and P = L, and largest at P = L/2, where the S-curve has its inflection point and steepest slope. The maximum rate is kL/4. Everything here (limit, max rate, inflection) can be read from the differential equation without solving it.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Set P₀ = 1, L = 8. When is the population growing fastest?

    What you should see

    When P = L/2 = 4 (the steepest part of the S, and the top of the parabola below). The maximum rate is kL/4 = 0.5·8/4 = 1.

  2. Start P₀ above L (try P₀ = 10, L = 8). What does P do?

    What you should see

    It decreases toward L: (1 − P/L) is negative, so the model pulls the population back down to the carrying capacity.

Worked example

dP/dt = 0.02P(1 − P/500), P(0) = 50. Find limt→∞ P, the P at which growth is fastest, and the fastest rate.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Compare with kP(1 − P/L). k = 0.02, L = 500.
  2. Limit. The carrying capacity: lim P = 500 (since 0 < P(0) < L).
  3. Fastest growth. At P = L/2 = 250.
  4. Rate. kL/4 = 0.02(500)/4 = 2.5 per time unit.
  5. Answer. Limit 500; fastest at P = 250 with rate 2.5.

Common mistakes

  • Using L = the limit of dP/dt. L is the limit of P itself.
  • Reading the maximum growth at P = L instead of L/2.
  • Forgetting the sign: for P > L the population decreases.
  • Confusing logistic with exponential growth.
  • Mixing up P (population) and dP/dt (growth rate) when reading graphs.

Solving it

Separating variables with partial fractions gives P(t) = L/(1 + Ae−kt) with A = (L − P₀)/P₀. You rarely need the formula on the exam: the equation itself tells you the limit, the equilibria and where growth is fastest.
