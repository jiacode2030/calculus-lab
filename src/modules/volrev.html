Volumes of revolution

Spin a region around an axis and it sweeps out a solid. Each slice is a disc (or a ring, called a washer), and its area is π × radius².

The idea

Slice the solid perpendicular to the axis of revolution. A slice through a region that touches the axis is a disc of radius R(x), area πR². If the region has a gap between it and the axis, the slice is a washer: outer radius R, inner radius r, area π(R² − r²). The volume is V = π ∫ab (R(x)² − r(x)²) dx.

Radii are distances from the axis. If you revolve around y = c, then R = (top − c) and r = (bottom − c). Square the distance, not the function: (f − g)² is not R² − r².

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose the first region. What shape is each slice, and what is its radius at x = 1?

    What you should see

    A disc with radius √1 = 1, area π. V = π∫₀⁴ x dx = 8π ≈ 25.13.

  2. Choose the region between √x and x². What is the slice now?

    What you should see

    A washer: outer radius √x, inner radius x². At x = 0.5 that is R ≈ 0.707 and r = 0.25. V = π∫₀¹ (x − x⁴) dx = 3π/10 ≈ 0.942.

  3. Back to the first region. Move the axis down to y = −1. What happens to each slice?

    What you should see

    Each slice gets a hole of radius 1 (the gap between the axis and the x-axis), and the outer radius grows to √x + 1. V = π(8 + 32/3) = 56π/3 ≈ 58.64.

  4. Now move the axis to y = −2. The region is the same. Why is the volume bigger?

    What you should see

    V = π(8 + 64/3) = 88π/3 ≈ 92.15. The region is farther from the axis, so every point travels around a bigger circle: the washers get wider rings, even though the hole grows too.

Worked example

Find the volume when the region under y = √x on [0, 4] is revolved about the x-axis, then about y = −1.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. About the x-axis (discs). R = √x, so V = π ∫04 x dx = π(8) = 8π.
  2. About y = −1 (washers). The axis is 1 unit below the x-axis: R = √x + 1, r = 1 (the x-axis is 1 above the axis).
  3. Set up. V = π ∫04 [(√x + 1)² − 1²] dx = π ∫04 (x + 2√x) dx.
  4. Integrate. π [x²/2 + (4/3)x3/2]04 = π(8 + 32/3).
  5. Answer. About x-axis: 8π. About y = −1: 56π/3.

Worked example 2: filling a bowl

The inside of a bowl is the curve y = x²/4 revolved about the y-axis (x and y in cm), and the bowl is 9 cm deep. (a) How much does the bowl hold? (b) How deep is the water when the bowl holds half that much? (c) Water is poured in at 20 cm³/s. How fast is the water level rising when the water is 4 cm deep?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Slice across the axis. The axis is vertical, so slice horizontally. At height y the slice is a disc of radius x, where y = x²/4 gives x² = 4y. Its area is πx² = 4πy.
  2. (a) Add up the discs. Water to depth h has volume V(h) = ∫0h 4πy dy = 2πh². Full: V(9) = 2π(81) = 162π ≈ 508.9 cm³.
  3. (b) Half full. 2πh² = 81π gives h² = 40.5, h = 9/√2 ≈ 6.36 cm. Half the volume sits in the top 29% of the depth, because the bowl is widest there.
  4. (c) Related rate. Differentiate V = 2πh² in t: dV/dt = 4πh·dh/dt. With dV/dt = 20 and h = 4: dh/dt = 20/(16π) ≈ 0.398 cm/s. (4πh is the area of the water's surface: the rise is the inflow spread over the surface.)
  5. Answers. (a) 162π ≈ 508.9 cm³, (b) about 6.36 cm, (c) about 0.398 cm/s, and it slows as the surface widens.

Common mistakes

  • Squaring the difference of functions: (f − g)² is not f² − g².
  • Forgetting π.
  • Measuring radii from the x-axis when the axis of revolution is a different line.
  • Using a disc when there is a gap (it should be a washer).
  • Mixing up outer and inner radius.
  • Revolving about the y-axis but still slicing in x: slices must be perpendicular to the axis, so integrate in y.

Which method?

If the slices perpendicular to the axis are full circles, use discs. If they have a hole, use washers. In AB you slice perpendicular to the axis of revolution (discs and washers); the shell method is not part of the course.
