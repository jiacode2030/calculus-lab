Volumes of revolution

Spin a region around an axis and it sweeps out a solid. Each slice is a disc (or a ring, called a washer), and its area is π × radius².

The idea

Slice the solid perpendicular to the axis of revolution. A slice through a region that touches the axis is a disc of radius R(x), area πR². If the region has a gap between it and the axis, the slice is a washer: outer radius R, inner radius r, area π(R² − r²). The volume is V = π ∫ab (R(x)² − r(x)²) dx.

Radii are distances from the axis. If you revolve around y = c, then R = (top − c) and r = (bottom − c). Square the distance, not the function: (f − g)² is not R² − r².

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose the first region. What shape is each slice, and what is its radius at x = 1?

    What you should see

    A disc with radius √1 = 1, area π. V = π∫₀⁴ x dx = 8π.

  2. Choose the region between √x and x². What is the slice now?

    What you should see

    A washer: outer radius √x, inner radius x². V = π∫₀¹ (x − x⁴) dx = 3π/10.

Worked example

Find the volume when the region under y = √x on [0, 4] is revolved about the x-axis, then about y = −1.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. About the x-axis (discs). R = √x, so V = π ∫04 x dx = π(8) = 8π.
  2. About y = −1 (washers). The axis is 1 unit below the x-axis: R = √x + 1, r = 1 (the x-axis is 1 above the axis).
  3. Set up. V = π ∫04 [(√x + 1)² − 1²] dx = π ∫04 (x + 2√x) dx.
  4. Integrate. π [x²/2 + (4/3)x3/2]04 = π(8 + 32/3).
  5. Answer. About x-axis: 8π. About y = −1: 56π/3.

Common mistakes

  • Squaring the difference of functions: (f − g)² is not f² − g².
  • Forgetting π.
  • Measuring radii from the x-axis when the axis of revolution is a different line.
  • Using a disc when there is a gap (it should be a washer).
  • Mixing up outer and inner radius.

Which method?

If the slices perpendicular to the axis are full circles, use discs. If they have a hole, use washers. In AB you slice perpendicular to the axis of revolution (discs and washers); the shell method is not part of the course.
