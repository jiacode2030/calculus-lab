Trig, e^x and ln x derivatives

Four functions to memorize once: the slopes of sine, cosine, ex and ln x.

The idea

Here is the pattern worth seeing. The slope of sin x is cos x: sine climbs fastest at 0 (slope 1 = cos 0) and is flat at its peaks (slope 0 = cos π/2). The slope of cos x is −sin x: cosine starts flat, then falls. The slope of ex is ex: the one function whose slope equals its height. The slope of ln x is 1/x: steep near 0, flatter and flatter after.

tan x, cot x, sec x and csc x follow from the quotient rule. The three with "co" in their names get minus signs. These derivatives need radians.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose sin x, find where the tangent is flat, and predict f′ there.

    What you should see

    At the peak (x = π/2) the tangent is flat, so f′ = 0, and cos(π/2) = 0. The slope graph is the cosine curve.

  2. Choose eˣ and tick the box. What do you notice about the two graphs?

    What you should see

    They are identical. Wherever the curve has height h its slope is h too. That is what makes e special.

Worked example

Differentiate f(x) = 3 sin x − 2ex + 5 ln x, and find f′(1).

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Term by term. (3 sin x)′ = 3 cos x; (−2ex)′ = −2ex; (5 ln x)′ = 5/x.
  2. Combine. f′(x) = 3 cos x − 2ex + 5/x.
  3. Evaluate at 1. 3 cos 1 − 2e + 5 = 3(0.5403) − 2(2.7183) + 5 = 1.6209 − 5.4366 + 5 ≈ 1.184 (radians).

Common mistakes

  • Writing (cos x)′ = sin x. The sign is minus: −sin x.
  • Using degrees on the calculator. The rules assume radians.
  • Writing (ex)′ = x ex−1. The power rule needs the variable in the base.
  • Writing (ln x)′ = ln x or x. It is 1/x.
  • Forgetting (ax)′ = ax ln a for other bases.

The other trig derivatives

(tan x)′ = sec²x, (cot x)′ = −csc²x, (sec x)′ = sec x tan x, (csc x)′ = −csc x cot x. You can get all of them from the quotient rule on sin and cos, but they are common enough to memorize.
