Series tests

geometric Σ arn = a/(1 − r)
only if |r| < 1

cannot prove convergence
p-series Σ 1/np converges iff p > 1

comparison: 0 ≤ an ≤ bn

converges; error ≤ first omitted term

< 1 converges · > 1 diverges · = 1 no info

    Four ideas you'll use everywhere

    • Zoom in far enough and a curve looks like a line. That line's slope is the derivative.
    • A derivative is a rate. Always ask: rate of what, per what?
    • An integral adds up thin slices. Area, distance, volume and total change are all the same move.
    • The two are opposites. The Fundamental Theorem says so, and it is why antiderivatives work.

    Which tool do I use?

    Most of the time lost on the exam goes into choosing a method. Learn the clues, then practice picking.

    The intuition

    Each problem type leaves clues. "Find the slope at a point" means a derivative. "Total change" or "area" means an integral. A 0/0 limit means factor, simplify or use L'Hôpital. "How fast is it changing" with two linked quantities means related rates. Name the method first; the algebra is the easy part.
